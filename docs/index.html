01The problem

You did the job at 2. You billed it at 11 p.m. Or never.

Your hands are wet. The next car is at the window. The next client is already on the table. So the paperwork becomes a second shift, the one nobody pays you for. By then you have forgotten half of it.

Every job you forget to bill is a job you did for free.

The model never touches the money

Prices are looked up, not guessed

Gemini only matches what you said to names on your price list. The price, the quantities and the total are computed in plain code.

A person confirms every bill

The ticket is shown and read back first. Only the raised fist, a double-tap or the Send button creates the invoice.

You can see where the money goes

The page names the Stripe account the bills are paid into. This build runs in Stripe's test mode only and refuses live keys.

Hard work should be paid the moment it is done.

Line cooks, plumbers, movers, nail techs, dog walkers, the kid working weekends at the drive-through. If you work with your hands, the paperwork should not need them too. TapJob listens, writes it down and gets you paid. Your evening is yours again.

Let your AI set it up

TapJob ships with notes written for AI coding agents. Hand yours this line: it can clone the code, run the tests and play a whole job on the pretend band. No band and no keys needed.

Clone https://github.com/HERO-1900/tapjob, read README.md and AGENTS.md, then run the demo with the pretend band and show me the paid ticket.

Signing you up to a hosted TapJob, with your own account and payouts, is the next step and is not built yet.

What comes next

  • Sign in and connect your own StripeToday you paste your own Stripe key. Next: one sign-in, and the money goes straight to your account.
  • Let your AI sign you upToday your AI can set TapJob up from the code. Next: tell it what you do, and it creates your hero, your price list and your account.
  • The phone appThe screens above, on iPhone and Android, talking to the band directly. A web page cannot reach a Bluetooth band on an iPhone.
  • The bill on the customer's phoneBy text message to a number you say out loud, or a pairing code the customer types.
  • Shake to undoA quick shake of the wrist takes the last ticket back, without touching a screen.
  • A shell for every brandThe band in your shop's colours, the hero in your uniform.
